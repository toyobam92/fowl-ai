Site snapshot

Google Analytics · Sep 7–13, 2026 (7-day window)

Biggest traffic week on record — and the worst conversion week. Active users 2.5×'d to 259 while key events fell 38 → 13, taking the user key-event rate from 31.4% to 4.6% and sitewide engagement rate from 50.0% to 29.9%. Deltas marked “vs prior” compare against Aug 17–23; unlike every comparison since Aug 8 that pair does not overlap — but there is a 14-day unmeasured gap between them (Aug 24 – Sep 6, no GA4 pull), so this is a three-week jump, not a week-over-week move. Two redesigns landed in or just before the window: “Signal” (Aug 28) and the unified visual identity (Sep 9, mid-window), so the post-redesign homepage read is split across two page titles and two designs. Read everything here as a first, confounded post-redesign look.

Bounce rate — what to fix, by page

GA4 bounce = no 10s+ engagement, no conversion event, no 2nd pageview. 259-user sample — the largest yet, so page-level numbers are firmer than usual, but the window straddles the Sep 9 redesign. “Prior” = Aug 17–23 (non-overlapping, but 14 unmeasured days apart).

Homepage
76%

Up from 64% prior and at the top of the page's all-time 58–78% band. 269 views / 211 users — the highest volume this page has ever had, and the worst bounce. First post-redesign read; the redesign is the only change that shipped, so this is the honest first data point against the “Signal” hypothesis. One read, mid-window design change — don't call it yet.

All Issues
8 views
Flat at 8 views / 6 users; bounce not captured (out of the top-7 card). Meanwhile individual issue pages pulled 17 views between them — the archive index is still the weaker entry point, which is the argument for the second half of TICKET-4 (next-issue links on issue pages).

What this window actually says: the site got 2.5× the traffic and converted a third as often. Engagement rate fell 50% → 30%, key events 38 → 13, Contact views 36 → 13, while homepage views nearly quadrupled to 269 at a 76% bounce. The most likely read is that the redesign plus the Vibe Code Saturdays push brought in a lot of shallow top-of-funnel traffic and simultaneously made the path to Contact less obvious — but two redesigns, a new high-traffic page, 15 sessions of the owner's own preview traffic, and a 14-day measurement gap all land in the same window, so no single cause is isolable. The standing rule from EXPERIMENTS.md still applies, now inverted: the sample is finally big enough that the conversion drop is hard to dismiss as noise, even though the page-level bounce moves still are.
